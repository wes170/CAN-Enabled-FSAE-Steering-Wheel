Four ways to get this wrong

Pairing D+ with D−

Tie A6–B6 and A7–B7. Not A6–A7.

Joining the two halves of each signal is what makes the connector reversible. Joining D+ to D− shorts the pair — a dead port that looks perfectly symmetrical on the schematic.

Grounding the SuperSpeed pads

They look like ten free pads to stitch down. They are not.

A full-featured cable carries live differential signals on those pins whenever a host attempts a SuperSpeed link, and grounding them loads the host's transmitter. Unused on this board is not unused on the cable.

Tabs straight to GND

All six go to NET_SHIELD, which reaches ground only through R11 ∥ C24.

Wire them to GND and you short out both parts, losing the shield isolation, on a board that still looks correct.

Reading the pad table as a grid

CC1 is A5; CC2 is B5. They are not adjacent.

The drawing prints the B row backwards, B12…B1. Read it as labelled pairs. Swapping the CC pins is harmless here — both are 5.1 kΩ to ground — but the same misreading one column over swaps SBU2 with CC2.

Why U5's VBUS pin goes to NET_VBUS, not NET_VBUS_OR

The clamp has to reference the node where the strike arrives. NET_VBUS is the connector side, unprotected, and that is where an ESD event lands. Referencing it to NET_VBUS_OR instead puts D6 in the discharge path — adding the Schottky's dynamic resistance in series with the thing whose whole job is to be a low-impedance shunt.

It is also what rigor rule 4 says in general: series impedance, filtering and clamp all go at the connector. (An earlier version of this diagram tapped it off NET_VBUS_OR — wrong, and corrected here.)

Two things this diagram cannot tell you

U5's pin numbers are not specified anywhere in the project. Every document describes it by function — I/O1, I/O2, VBUS, GND — and none gives a pin number for the SOT-23-6 package. That is the same gap as the BAV199's third pin, and it is recorded rather than guessed: st.com and the LCSC mirror both failed to return the datasheet. Take the pin mapping from the library symbol and confirm it against ST's datasheet before capture.

Fitting R_VBUS lets USB reach the vehicle rail. VBUS works back through the buck's high-side body diode onto +12V_P at roughly 4 V, turning Q1 on and putting about 4 V on J1 pin 1. Harmless with J1 unmated, which is the bench case. Accepted deliberately — a blocking diode would cost 0.4 V and the CAN transceiver needs 4.75 V minimum.
